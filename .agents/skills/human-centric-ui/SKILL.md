# Human-Centric UI & Product Design Principles

A human-centric interface is designed for real people—with limited attention, emotional vulnerability, and a desire for clarity and mastery. It avoids corporate jargon, generic AI templates, and cynical dark patterns.

---

## 1. The Design Masters Synthesis

### A. Dieter Rams (10 Principles of Good Design)
1. **Good design is innovative:** Technological novelty is only worthwhile if it serves human utility.
2. **Good design makes a product useful:** It emphasizes the utility of an interface while disregarding anything that detracts from it.
3. **Good design is aesthetic:** The aesthetic quality of a tool is an integral part of its utility because tools affect our well-being.
4. **Good design makes a product understandable:** It clarifies the product's structure. Better still, it makes the product talk.
5. **Good design is unobtrusive:** Software interfaces are neither decorative objects nor artworks; their design should be neutral and restrained.
6. **Good design is honest:** It does not make a product appear more innovative, powerful, or valuable than it really is.
7. **Good design is long-lasting:** Avoids being fashionable and therefore never appears antiquated.
8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail:** Nothing must be arbitrary or left to chance. Care and accuracy in design reflect respect towards the user.
9. **Good design is environmentally friendly:** Conserves resources and visual bandwidth.
10. **Good design is as little design as possible ("Weniger, aber besser"):** Concentrates on the essential aspects rather than non-essentials. Back to purity, back to simplicity.

### B. Swiss Style (Josef Müller-Brockmann & International Typographic Style)
* **Mathematical Grid Rhythm:** Structural unity where all modules, columns, and gutters follow an unyielding vertical and horizontal rhythm.
* **Asymmetric Equilibrium:** Creating dynamic tension through offset alignments, large typographic statements balanced by compact, high-density data panels.
* **Typographic Hierarchy as Architecture:** Using weight (Light, Regular, SemiBold, Black) and scale ratios (12 / 14 / 18 / 24 / 36 / 56px) rather than conflicting colors to communicate importance.

### C. Refactoring UI (Steve Schoger & Adam Wathan)
* **Design with Contrast First, Color Second:** Perfect your layout in grayscale to ensure clear hierarchy before introducing accent colors.
* **Semantic Depth & Shadows:** Use multiple ambient box shadows with low opacity to simulate real physical depth instead of muddy single shadows.
* **Establish Deliberate Spacing Systems:** Multiples of 4px and 8px (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96px).

### D. Don Norman (Human-Centered Design)
* **Discoverability & Conceptual Models:** The user can immediately figure out what actions are possible and where they are.
* **Affordances and Signifiers:** Physical and visual signals communicate how to operate an interface without reading a manual.
* **Immediate Feedback:** Real-time visual and haptic confirmation of every state change.

### E. Basecamp & 37signals (Conversational Warmth & Calm Tech)
* Write like a smart, empathetic friend speaking plainly.
* No corporate buzzwords ("enterprise synergies", "frictionless paradigm shifts").
* Respect the learner's attention; create calm, unhurried digital environments.

### F. Duolingo & Behavioral Psychology
* **Loss Aversion:** Protect habit momentum with streak shields and streak freezes.
* **Endowed Progress:** Start users with meaningful initial wins so they feel momentum from day one.
* **Tactile Gamification:** Satisfying pushable buttons, crown mastery, and clear skill progression paths.
